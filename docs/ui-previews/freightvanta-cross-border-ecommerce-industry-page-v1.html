THE OPERATING DECISION

A customer order is only as reliable as the decisions made before it exists.

A storefront can make a product look ready long before the operating path is ready. For products sourced from China, that path crosses supplier questions, approved variants, packaging components, inbound expectations, inventory identification, sales-channel rules, and a final customer-order instruction.

FreightVanta brings those decisions into one working path, so your team can see what is ready, what needs approval, and what should happen next.

  • Product version, quantity, and destination remain attached to the next instruction.
  • Approved packaging becomes an order-level rule, not a loose folder of files.
  • Inbound stock has a purpose and release owner before it is treated as available.
  • DTC, marketplace, retail, and replenishment routes remain distinct operational contexts.

SALES CHANNELS

Set the rule around the way you actually sell.

One product can have one source and several routes to market. Make their operating requirements visible before stock or packaging instructions are reused in the wrong context.

DTC STOREFRONT

Your order experience is part of the brand promise.

Product protection, inserts, bundles, customer-facing language, and the active packout version need to stay connected when the customer receives the order.

MARKETPLACE

Channel requirements need their own preparation rule.

A listing, replenishment, carton instruction, prep requirement, or destination can differ from a DTC order. Record that context rather than treating all stock as automatically ready.

WHOLESALE / RETAIL

The receiving handoff is not the same as a consumer order.

Item identifiers, carton details, delivery instructions, documents, and bundle composition may belong in a retail route but not in a direct-to-customer packout.

INVENTORY WITH CONTEXT

A SKU is not ready because it has a name and a price.

Before a product moves through a cross-border ecommerce operation, the next team needs more than a storefront title. They need an active product version, product reference, packaging context, channel purpose, and an owner for exceptions.

  • Product reference, variation, dimensions, material, and approved version.
  • Known supplier or sender, expected quantity, carton context, and ready period.
  • Target market, sales channel, and stock purpose.
  • Active packout version, component quantities, and language variants.
  • Inbound identifier, discrepancy owner, and release authorization.
