# FreightVanta Cross-Border E-commerce Industry Solution

## 1. Page Decision

Create a dedicated English industry-solution page for ecommerce operators who buy products in China and need the work after purchase to remain connected: product information, branded packaging, inbound inventory, order preparation, delivery handoff, and operational exceptions.

This is not another generic service page and not a list of six FreightVanta services. The reader should see a single ecommerce operating path, then understand which service can support each handoff. The page must help a brand, importer, marketplace seller, or ecommerce operations lead decide what information to prepare before requesting a scope.

**Proposed route:** `/industries/cross-border-ecommerce`  
**Navigation label:** `Industries` -> `Cross-border ecommerce`  
**Breadcrumb:** `Home / Industries / Cross-border ecommerce`  
**Primary conversion:** `Request an ecommerce operating plan`  
**Secondary conversion:** `See the connected workflow`

### Audience

- Ecommerce founders and operations leads preparing an initial or repeat product launch.
- Marketplace, DTC, and retail teams sourcing products from China.
- Importers coordinating suppliers, packaging components, inventory, and more than one sales channel.
- Brand managers who need customer-facing packaging to stay connected to operational instructions.

### Positioning

> FreightVanta helps ecommerce teams organize the operational handoffs that follow product sourcing from China. The focus is a usable plan, not unsupported claims about price, speed, warehouse coverage, carrier access, automatic marketplace integrations, or delivery outcomes.

### Module 8: FAQ - Questions ecommerce teams need answered before an enquiry

**Layout:** white, asymmetric 35/65 split. Keep the contextual heading to the left and the six accessible native disclosure rows to the right. First FAQ opens by default. Do not hide important copy behind a JavaScript-only accordion.

**H2:**

> Scope questions, answered before the operational conversation starts.

1. **Can we start with only a product link or product image?**  
   Yes. A link, image, rough SKU list, or short specification can be enough to start a source-to-operation conversation. The resulting plan should identify the missing product, packaging, quantity, channel, and destination information instead of guessing it.

2. **Does this page promise direct integration with our ecommerce platform or marketplace?**  
   No. Storefront, marketplace, OMS, WMS, order-data, and tracking connections depend on confirmed technical and operational scope. The enquiry should collect the relevant platform, order source, fields, current workflow, and target outcome before an integration is described as available.

3. **Can products sourced from China be prepared for both DTC and marketplace sales?**  
   They can be planned around more than one approved route, but the product version, available inventory, packaging, prep requirement, destination, and authorization rule must be clear for each route. A common source does not make every unit automatically ready for every channel.

4. **Does ecommerce fulfillment include returns, carrier selection, or delivery guarantees?**  
   Do not assume that it does. Returns policy, product acceptance, carrier options, transit time, tracking, rate, country coverage, and exception ownership need confirmation against the actual product, destinations, and operating scope.

5. **Can packaging and branded inserts be coordinated with sourced products?**  
   Yes, the approved packaging and branding decisions can be organized into a packout brief. Design ownership, component manufacturing, legal copy, regulated labels, and stock-management terms remain subject to confirmation.

6. **What should we prepare for a planning conversation?**  
   Provide the product reference, source status, estimated quantity, target market, intended sales channels, packaging requirements, expected next handoff, and the decisions that are still open. A useful plan begins by making the unknowns visible.

## 5. Motion, Accessibility, And Responsive Rules

### Motion

- If Three.js and GSAP remain part of the public-site system, use them only for the hero's subtle route-line progression and a very light depth shift in the two image areas. The information must remain fully visible with JavaScript disabled.
- Stage labels may fade in once, in reading order, but no content can begin hidden.
- Use `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` to disable movement and render the rail as a static line.
- Do not add a perpetual spinning globe, particle field, floating boxes, pulsing circles, or data visualizations that imply unverified live tracking.

### Responsive layout

- Desktop container: use the existing FreightVanta maximum content width and 64px side gutters.
- Tablet: hero becomes a text block followed by a 16:10 image; the workflow changes from three columns by two rows to two columns by three rows.
- Mobile: all editorial splits stack; preserve the hero image at a fixed `4 / 5` ratio and the inbound image at `3 / 4`; never use an image's intrinsic height to expand the page.
- Scenario and related-service rows stack their labels above copy; they remain border-separated rows rather than turning into detached cards.
- The FAQ summaries support long questions without clipping and retain a visible keyboard focus style.
- Body text must meet WCAG AA contrast; light text over photography needs a solid or sufficiently opaque navy overlay, not a low-contrast shadow.
